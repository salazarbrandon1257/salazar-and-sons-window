Most popular in DFW

Vinyl

Low maintenance and energy efficient, which is why most homeowners choose it.

Best for: Most homes, and replacing old aluminum windows

Why people choose it

  • Never needs painting or sealing
  • Hollow, chambered frames insulate well
  • Won't rot, swell, or rust
  • Strong performance for the cost

Good to know

  • Quality varies a lot: cheap vinyl can warp or fade in Texas sun
  • Look for welded corners and reinforced frames
  • Fewer colors (white, almond, clay; some lines offer black)

Fiberglass & composite

Strong, stable frames that hold their shape in Texas heat. Both cost more than vinyl.

Best for: Darker colors, slim frames, or the look of wood with little upkeep

Ask us about Pella Impervia (fiberglass) and the Andersen 100 Series (Fibrex composite).

Why people choose it

  • Fiberglass expands and contracts about as much as glass, so seals stay tight
  • Strong enough for slimmer frames and more glass
  • Fiberglass can be painted almost any color
  • Composite blends wood fiber and polymer: resists rot and holds its shape

Good to know

  • Costs more than vinyl
  • Fewer manufacturers and styles to choose from

Aluminum

Slim and strong. We only install aluminum with a thermal break, an insulating barrier that keeps the metal from carrying heat inside.

Best for: Modern designs where thin frames matter most

Why people choose it

  • Very strong with thin, modern frames
  • Durable and low maintenance

Good to know

  • Still less energy efficient than vinyl or fiberglass
  • Can still collect some condensation on very cold days
  • Many older DFW homes still have single-pane aluminum windows, and replacing them is one of the biggest energy upgrades you can make

Wood

Classic and warm, with the most upkeep.

Best for: Historic homes where the look comes first

Ask us about aluminum-clad wood windows: the Andersen E-Series and Pella Reserve.

Why people choose it

  • Traditional look that can be stained or painted
  • Natural insulator

Good to know

  • Needs regular painting or sealing
  • Can rot or swell if moisture gets in
  • Usually the most expensive option
  • Often "clad" with aluminum or vinyl outside to cut upkeep

The frame is only half the story

Low-E glass

A thin, invisible coating that reflects the sun's heat, keeping your home cooler in a Texas summer.

Argon gas

Fills the space between panes to slow heat transfer.
